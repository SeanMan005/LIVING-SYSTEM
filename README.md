# Five Marks of a Living System

An illustrated essay proposing that "living" is defined by how a system behaves, not by what it's made of.

**Live page:** https://seanman005.github.io/LIVING-SYSTEM/

## The main idea

Life isn't defined by cells, carbon, or biology. It's defined by a set of behaviors. Anything that shows all five, whether a brain, a society, a microbial colony, or a network of machines, counts as a living system.

## The five marks

1. **It learns from a feedback loop.** It takes in information, builds an internal model, acts on it, sees the result, and corrects itself.
2. **It needs fresh input from the outside world.** Cut it off, and it starts feeding on its own outputs, drifting further from reality until it breaks down. This is the decisive test.
3. **It alternates between exploring and refining.** It generates new possibilities, then uses feedback to cut them down to what works. Only exploring makes it chaotic. Only refining makes it rigid.
4. **Its gains compound.** It turns input into capability that builds on itself over time. Its power comes from how much of the world it touches multiplied by how well it processes what it takes in.
5. **It actively keeps itself organized.** It spends energy filtering out disorder, the way an immune system rejects threats, instead of slowly falling apart.

## Quick test

| Mark | Ask |
|---|---|
| Feedback loop | Can you trace a path from what it senses, to what it stores, to what it does, and back? |
| Fresh input | If you cut off the outside world, does it degrade? |
| Explore and refine | Does it alternate, or is it stuck in one mode? |
| Compounding | Does each cycle's output make the next cycle bigger? |
| Self-maintenance | Is it spending energy to stay organized? |

## Related ideas

- **Cybernetics and homeostasis:** systems that regulate themselves through feedback
- **Autopoiesis** (Maturana and Varela): living systems as self-producing and self-maintaining
- **Schrödinger's *What Is Life?*:** life as a system that holds off disorder by drawing order from its environment
- **Predictive processing:** the brain as a prediction machine that corrects itself against incoming signals
- **Model collapse:** AI models trained on their own outputs degrade over time
- **Swarm intelligence:** colonies with no central brain that still solve complex problems
